Detailed Category Overview

Below 18.5

Underweight

A BMI below 18.5 indicates that a person may not be consuming enough nutrients or could have an underlying condition affecting weight. It may be associated with lowered immunity, nutritional deficiencies, and fatigue.

18.5 to below 25

Healthy Weight

A BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 is considered optimal for most adult men and women. It is correlated with lower incidence of chronic conditions such as cardiovascular disease, hypertension, and type 2 diabetes.

25 to below 30

Overweight

A BMI between 25.0 and 29.9 indicates body weight above the standard threshold for height. While it may increase the risk of cardiovascular stress, individuals with high muscle mass can also fall into this range.

30 and above

Obesity

A BMI of 30.0 or higher is classified as obesity. Healthcare providers commonly evaluate lifestyle factors, blood pressure, lipid panels, and metabolic markers to recommend customized wellness and nutritional plans.

Important Limitations of BMI

Body Mass Index (BMI) does not directly measure body fat or distinguish between muscle, bone density, and adipose tissue. As a consequence, athletes and bodybuilders with high muscle mass may be classified as overweight or obese, while older adults who have lost muscle tissue may register in the healthy range despite carrying excess fat. BMI should always be viewed as a high-level screening indicator rather than a diagnostic evaluation.
