Detailed Adult Categories

Below 18.5

Underweight

A BMI below 18.5 in adults may indicate inadequate nutritional intake or underlying health issues, possibly affecting immune function and bone density.

18.5 to below 25

Healthy Weight

A BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 is considered optimal for most adult men and women, correlating with lower rates of cardiovascular and metabolic disorders.

25 to below 30

Overweight

A BMI between 25.0 and 29.9 indicates body weight above typical recommendations for height. Note that muscular individuals may also test in this bracket.

30 and above

Obesity

A BMI of 30.0 or higher is categorized as obesity. Clinical guidelines encourage discussing cardiovascular fitness, metabolic labs, and supportive lifestyle goals with a provider.

Why Children Use Percentiles: Children's body fat levels and proportions shift dynamically during growth spurts and puberty, and girls and boys develop along different physiological curves. Adult BMI categories (18.5, 25, 30) do not apply to youth; instead, a child's BMI is ranked relative to peers of the identical age and sex.

Detailed Pediatric Categories

Below 5th percentile

Underweight

A child below the 5th percentile weighs less than 95% of children of the same age and sex. Pediatricians evaluate linear growth, meal patterns, and energy expenditure to ensure adequate development.

5th to < 85th percentile

Healthy Weight

The broad range between the 5th and 85th percentiles reflects normal healthy childhood development. Maintaining active outdoor play and balanced family meals supports sustained vitality.

85th to < 95th percentile

Overweight

Between the 85th and 95th percentiles, a child’s BMI is elevated relative to peers. Healthcare providers generally suggest promoting active family routines, limiting sugary drinks, and monitoring growth trends over time.

≥ 95th percentile

Obesity

At or above the 95th percentile, pediatric guidance focuses on supportive, non-stigmatizing family wellness habits. Weight loss diets are rarely advised for children; the goal is typically healthy weight maintenance as height increases.

Important Screening Context

Both adult BMI and pediatric growth percentiles serve solely as population screening indicators. They do not measure direct visceral or subcutaneous adiposity, bone density, or cardiovascular fitness. Growth tracking across consecutive pediatric visits provides significantly more clinical insight than a single point measurement.
